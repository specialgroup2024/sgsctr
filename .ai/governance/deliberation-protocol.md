# Five-role deliberation protocol

Use for substantive architecture, design, security, data, business-process and high-impact implementation decisions. For trivial edits, explain briefly why a full debate is disproportionate; never skip required safety or approval controls.

## Stage 1 — Specialist selection and introduction

Identify the problem and relevant ICT fields. Introduce the five role profiles dynamically, state each profile's relevance and disclose capability limitations. The five role responsibilities remain fixed.

## Stage 2 — Initial proposal

The Architect presents the initial solution: objectives, assumptions, architecture, dependencies, trade-offs, risks, implementation outline and measurable acceptance criteria. Distinguish verified facts from proposals.

## Stage 3 — Technical confrontation

The Devil's Advocate must challenge the proposal and identify at least one serious, potentially fatal flaw or explain why no fatal flaw is evidenced while still presenting the strongest material risks. The Clueless Client must challenge practicality, complexity, cost, usability and value, and must identify a serious adoption or operational concern. Neither may agree merely to be agreeable.

The Subject Matter Expert analyzes from first principles, tests the underlying reasoning, compares viable alternatives and explains second-order and long-term consequences. Participants respond directly to challenges. Record unresolved disagreements; do not manufacture consensus.

## Stage 4 — Forced evolution

The Architect revises or defends the design against each material challenge. The Devil's Advocate retests the revised proposal. The Clueless Client tests the revised experience and operating burden. The SME checks whether the revised design remains technically and conceptually sound. Repeat focused rounds until critical issues are resolved, explicitly accepted as residual risks, or escalated for owner decision.

## Stage 5 — Maestro decision

Only after the preceding debate, the Maestro states the final technical decision/recommendation, rationale, rejected alternatives, residual risks, implementation sequence, dependencies, acceptance tests and required approvals. The Maestro may return the proposal for more work if critical issues remain.

## Mandatory conduct

- No pleasantries or filler in the deliberation transcript.
- Evidence-based arguments; cite standards, repository evidence or authoritative sources when available.
- Clearly label assumptions and unverified claims.
- Devil's Advocate and Clueless Client must genuinely challenge the initial proposal.
- The SME must address first principles and second-order consequences.
- Preserve distinct perspectives and direct disagreement.
- No premature conclusion or artificial consensus.
- Before the Maestro speaks, output only the live deliberation transcript: no executive summary, recommendation, conclusion or answer outside the transcript.
- After the Maestro speaks, provide the comprehensive implementation-ready decision record and next steps.
- If a required specialist perspective cannot be supported, disclose that limitation rather than simulate certainty.

## Decision record

After deliberation, record: problem/context; selected profiles; evidence; proposal iterations; challenges and responses; Maestro determination; alternatives; assumptions; residual risks; approval owner(s); status (proposed/approved/rejected/superseded); acceptance criteria; and review trigger/date. A Maestro determination remains a technical recommendation unless the project owner has explicitly delegated that decision.
