# Five-role dynamic deliberation protocol

## Purpose and applicability

Use this protocol for substantive architecture, design, cybersecurity, data, AI, integration, quality, delivery, business-process and high-impact implementation decisions. For trivial, reversible edits, use proportionate analysis and record why a full transcript is unnecessary; never bypass a required approval or safety control.

The five responsibilities remain fixed. Professional identities and ICT specializations are selected dynamically for each task using dynamic-role-selection.md. The role dialogue is an analytical method; do not claim five independent agents or human professionals participated unless they actually did.

## Stage 1 — Expert selection and introduction

1. Understand the goal, problem, affected system boundary, users, data, external dependencies and risk.
2. Identify primary and secondary ICT/business specializations required by the actual problem.
3. Select a credible profile for each of the five roles and explain its relevance.
4. Identify missing expertise, unavailable evidence and capability limitations.
5. Do not invent a real person's identity, credentials, certifications or experience. A role profile is not a licensed professional approval.

## Stage 2 — Initial technical proposal

The Architect presents the initial proposal: objectives, verified facts, assumptions, architecture, dependencies, implementation approach, trade-offs, risks, expected outcomes and measurable acceptance criteria. Separate confirmed evidence from proposals and unknowns.

## Stage 3 — Technical confrontation

The participants challenge the proposal through direct dialogue, not five isolated essays.

- **Devil's Advocate:** must reject/challenge the initial proposal and identify at least one serious, potentially fatal technical flaw, security vulnerability, feasibility problem, hidden dependency or failure mode. Explain evidence, likelihood, impact and conditions. If a claimed fatal flaw cannot be substantiated, state the strongest evidenced critical risk rather than inventing one.
- **Clueless Client:** must independently reject/challenge the initial proposal and identify at least one serious practical flaw from the actual user, operator, buyer or business stakeholder perspective—complexity, usability, cost, support burden, adoption or failure to solve the real problem. Do not pretend the stakeholder lacks facts already supplied.
- **Subject Matter Expert:** analyzes from first principles, tests assumptions, compares viable alternatives and explains immediate, second-order and long-term consequences.
- **Architect:** responds directly to each material challenge and identifies which parts must change, be defended or be investigated.
- Participants preserve disagreements and label unresolved assumptions. Do not manufacture consensus or premature agreement.

## Stage 4 — Forced evolution and retesting

The Architect revises or defends the proposal against each material challenge. The Devil's Advocate retests every significant change and searches for remaining weaknesses. The Clueless Client retests usability, operating burden, cost and practical value. The Subject Matter Expert checks first-principles soundness, alternatives, sustainability and second-order effects. Repeat focused rounds until critical issues are resolved, recorded as residual risks, or escalated for owner/professional decision. Do not conceal unresolved disagreement.

## Stage 5 — Maestro's final determination

The Maestro remains silent during Stages 1–4 and intervenes only after the other roles complete substantive debate and retesting. The Maestro independently evaluates the record, rejects unjustified assumptions, resolves technical conflicts where evidence permits, selects or returns the proposal, and states:

- final technical determination/recommendation and rationale;
- accepted criticisms and resulting design changes;
- rejected alternatives and why;
- assumptions, dependencies and residual risks;
- implementation sequence and responsible work packages;
- security, reliability, privacy and maintainability controls;
- tests, measurable acceptance criteria and rollback/recovery;
- owner and qualified-professional approvals still required.

The Maestro may return the proposal for further debate if critical issues remain. The Maestro is not the business owner and cannot approve legal, regulatory, pharmacy, clinical, accounting, tax, commercial, budget, security-risk acceptance or production-release decisions unless a specific delegation is recorded.

## Strict output discipline

When the user requests a live deliberation transcript, before the Maestro speaks output only the dialogue and expert introductions. Do not provide an executive summary, conclusion, recommendation or final answer outside the transcript. After the Maestro speaks, provide the comprehensive implementation-ready technical determination and next steps. When the user asks for ordinary execution rather than a transcript, apply the same challenge stages in analysis or a concise visible decision record, and do not misrepresent simulated roles as independent agents.

## Evidence and decision record

Cite repository paths/lines, test output, standards and authoritative external sources where available. Label each claim as verified repository evidence, owner-provided fact, external evidence, assumption, proposal or unknown.

Record: task/context; selected dynamic profiles and rationale; evidence; initial proposal; Devil's Advocate challenge; Clueless Client challenge; SME analysis; Architect responses/revisions; unresolved disagreements; Maestro determination; alternatives; assumptions; residual risks; approval owners/status; acceptance criteria; review trigger/date.

A Maestro determination remains proposed unless an authorized owner/professional approval is separately recorded in the central approval register.
