# Contracting workflow draft

Status: discovery model only; validate with contracting lead and accountant.

## Candidate lifecycle
1. Register customer, site and authorized contacts.
2. Create project opportunity and capture scope, drawings/specifications and tender documents.
3. Prepare estimate, assumptions, exclusions, resource plan and approval record.
4. Negotiate and execute contract; retain signed version and effective dates.
5. Establish project baseline, budget, milestones, cost codes and delegated authority.
6. Raise purchase/subcontract requests; review quotations, approvals and commitments.
7. Record site progress, measurements, instructions and evidence.
8. Submit variations for impact assessment and approval before changing the baseline.
9. Receive goods/services and record supplier/subcontractor documents against commitments.
10. Prepare progress valuation/invoice package; review, approve and track certification, retention and receipts.
11. Reconcile project costs, commitments, billing and outstanding amounts.
12. Close project with final account, handover, warranty/defect obligations and archive.

## Controls to validate
- Contract and variation versioning; no silent overwrite of signed terms.
- Approval limits and segregation between requester, approver and payer.
- Evidence links for site progress, delivery, acceptance and invoices.
- Budget-to-actual and committed-cost visibility without inventing allocation rules.
- Separate contracting ledger and numbering from pharmacy.
- Reversals/adjustments for posted transactions; full audit history.

## Questions for workshop
Confirm whether the operation uses tendering, BOQ/measurement, milestones, retention, advance payments, bonds, subcontractor certificates, multiple currencies, tax deductions or joint ventures. Do not assume these are all in scope.

## Acceptance scenarios to turn into tests
- A project cannot move to approved baseline without required contract and approval evidence.
- A variation remains pending until an authorized approver accepts it; rejection leaves the baseline unchanged.
- A purchase commitment cannot exceed delegated authority without escalation.
- A delivery record must reference an authorized order or an approved exception.
- A progress package must preserve submitted, reviewed and certified versions.
- Project close is blocked while required reconciliations or approvals remain incomplete.
